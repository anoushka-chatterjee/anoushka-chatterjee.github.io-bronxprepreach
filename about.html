Community-informed systems science

About the Project

Bronx PrEP REACH is a community-engaged research project that brings together community expertise and systems science to identify effective strategies for improving HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) initiation and sustained use in the Bronx, New York.

At the center of our research is a system dynamics model designed to examine how interconnected social, structura and healthcare-related factors influence PrEP engagement. By simulating different intervention strategies, the model allows us to evaluate their potential impact on PrEP initiation and retention, helping identify promising approaches for improving access to and continuity of PrEP services.

To develop this model, we collaborate with a Community and Implementer Taskforce composed of individuals who actively work in HIV prevention, healthcare delivery, and community outreach throughout the Bronx. Through a series of participatory group model building (GMB) sessions, taskforce members share their firsthand experiences and identify gaps in PrEP education, healthcare access, service delivery, and ongoing engagement in care.

Their expertise helps us identify important variables and relationships within the system, better understand populations that may be underserved by existing services, and determine where interventions may have the greatest impact.

By integrating community knowledge with quantitative modeling, Bronx PrEP REACH aims to identify implementation strategies that are not only potentially effective but also practical, community-informed, and responsive to the needs of the populations they are designed to serve.

The challenge

Why PrEP reach matters

Pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) is an effective HIV prevention option, but reaching people who could benefit from PrEP—and supporting initiation and sustained use—requires attention to interconnected social, clinical, and structural factors.

In the Bronx, this project brings community partners and researchers together to examine how these factors interact and where implementation strategies may have the greatest impact.

Our approach

Community knowledge informs the model

PARTICIPATION

Group model building

Taskforce members share lived, professional, and community knowledge to identify barriers, connections, and potential leverage points.

SIMULATION

System dynamics modeling

Insights from the sessions inform a model that explores how PrEP engagement may change over time under different strategies.

Research priorities

Three connected aims

AIM 1

Build and validate

Develop and validate a system dynamics model of factors influencing PrEP reach in the Bronx.

AIM 2

Identify and evaluate

Use the model to examine promising strategies to improve PrEP reach.

AIM 3

Pilot implementation

Pilot high-potential engagement strategies identified through the modeling process.
